Day 1. One lily pad.

Here’s a question.

A patch of lily pads is growing across a pond.

Scroll when you’re ready.

Every day, the patch doubles.

One becomes two. Two become four. Four become eight.

It keeps doubling for 30 days.

At first, almost nothing seems to change.

On Day 30, the pond is completely covered.

Now think about this.

When was the pond half covered?

Don’t calculate yet. Pick a day in your head.

Hold that answer for a second.

Somewhere around Day 15… right?

Half of 30 is 15. It feels like the obvious answer.

But no. It’s Day 29.

Go back just one day from full. On Day 29, the pond is exactly 50% covered.

One more day changes everything.

Day 29 → 50%. Day 30 → 100%. Half doubled once becomes full.

We do this in life too.

We work for a long time, see only a little progress, and start thinking it isn’t working.

Don’t quit at Day 28.

At Day 28, this pond is only 25% covered. Two doublings later, it is full. Progress can feel the same: slow for a long time, then suddenly obvious. Give the work enough time to compound.
